    Agentic Incident Resolution for ServiceNow

    Demo paper for the SIGHT team reviewBuilt on development @ 14100f0September 2026
    Abstract When a ServiceNow incident is created, our agent reads it, decides how risky it is, searches the knowledge base, and writes a cited fix back to the ticket exactly once. When the risk is high or the evidence is weak, it stops and asks a human. Every approved human fix becomes a new knowledge article, so the next similar ticket is solved instantly. The paper has five parts, one per presenter; each part lists the screens that presenter opens.

    Idea, problem and how we built it

    1.1The idea

    An agent that resolves ServiceNow incidents on its own, and knows when it must not.

    ResolveClassify, search, write a cited fix, once.
    Stop when unsureHigh risk or weak evidence goes to a human.
    LearnApproved fixes become reusable KB articles.

    1.2The problem

    IT teams solve the same incidents again and again. The answer is usually already in the knowledge base, but a person still has to read the ticket, search and type the fix. AI helps only if it can be trusted inside a live ITSM system.

    Slow first responseTickets wait in a queue before anyone reads them.
    Repeated workThe same fix is searched for and retyped every time.
    TrustAn AI writing into ServiceNow needs state, least privilege and an audit trail.
    Lost knowledgeAn engineer's fix stays in one ticket and is never reused.

    2.3Inside the agent

    The agent is a graph of small steps. Risk is decided before retrieval, so a dangerous ticket costs no search and no model spend. After retrieval, more gates can still stop the run.

    StageWhat it does
    load · validateRead the ticket, wrap prompt injection, redact secrets
    classify · riskCategory and risk level; high risk pauses here
    retrieveHybrid search over the ServiceNow KB; reuse a strong KBHR match
    diagnose · generate · criticThree agents: find the cause, write a cited fix, check it against evidence
    safety · confidenceOutput guardrails, then the 0.6 confidence floor
    review brief · humanBuild the approval brief and wait for a decision
    act · capture KBHRWrite once to ServiceNow; publish an approved human fix as a KB article

    Reliability, tracing and next steps

    5.4Next steps

    Everything above works today. These are the next four pieces we would add. Each card says what it is, why it matters, and how we would build it on what already exists.

    1. Analytics dashboard approved

    What
    A page with engineer hours saved, cost per incident, auto-resolved vs sent to a human (and why), and how often KBHR articles are reused.
    Why
    Turns the demo into a business number: “the agent saved X hours this week”.
    How
    Built from data we already store: runs and approvals in Postgres, token cost in Langfuse.

    2. Guardrail gate before classify

    What
    An extra layer between validate and classify that screens the ticket before anything is sent to the LLM. If screening does not finish or fails, classification does not run and the ticket goes to a human.
    Why
    Today redaction happens inside validate. A separate gate guarantees no text reaches the model unchecked, even if a step errors.
    How
    A new graph node with its own route: pass → classify, blocked or incomplete → review brief. Logged as its own Langfuse span.

    3. Smarter risk

    What
    Check risk again after retrieval, and show similar approved cases inside the approval brief.
    Why
    Some risks only show once we see the evidence, and reviewers decide faster with past examples.
    How
    A second risk step after retrieve; reuse KBHR search results in the brief.

    4. Full incident view with actions

    What
    One screen per incident: AI fields, work notes, every log row, the KB article used and the approval history, plus two buttons: Retry to run the agent again on a failed incident, and Delete to remove a test incident.
    Why
    An engineer sees what the AI did and can act on it from one place, without opening four tabs.
    How
    Combine the existing executions, trace and ServiceNow read endpoints. Retry reuses the dead-letter replay (operator only); Delete reuses the existing dashboard delete endpoint, with a confirm step.
    “Event-driven, guarded, observable, and it learns from every human approval. Thank you.”
